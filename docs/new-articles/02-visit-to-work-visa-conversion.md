## Article body

### [PARAGRAPH] Intro

Converting a UAE visit visa to a work visa without leaving the country used to be a paperwork ordeal that required an out-of-country status change to Oman or Iran. As of the latest ICP and MOHRE updates, in-country conversion is now the standard path — and the whole timeline can be wrapped up in 7–14 working days if your employer moves fast. This guide walks through the exact 2026 process, what each fee actually costs, what documents the employer needs from you, and the failure points that send candidates back to step one.

### [PARAGRAPH] Quick takeaways bullet list

- In-country status change is now allowed for most visit-visa holders. You do not need to fly to Oman or Kish.
- The conversion is **employer-led** — you cannot start the process on your own.
- The end-to-end cost (paid by the employer in most cases) is around AED 5,500–8,500 depending on emirate, profession, and free zone vs mainland.
- The minimum timeline is **7 working days**. Realistic is **10–14**.
- A failed medical fitness test (HIV, Hepatitis B, TB, syphilis) is the most common showstopper. There is no appeal — you must exit the country.

## [H2] When you can convert (and when you can't)

You can convert your UAE visit visa to a work visa in-country if:

- You hold a valid tourist or visit visa that has at least **10 days remaining** before expiry (some emirates want 30 days — Dubai is stricter than Sharjah).
- You have a **firm job offer** from a UAE employer with an active establishment card and MOHRE quota.
- You pass the **medical fitness test** required for residence.
- You are **not on a UAE entry ban** (employment ban, immigration ban, or court order).

You cannot convert if:

- You overstayed your visit visa even by one day. You must clear overstay fines and either renew the visit visa or exit and re-enter.
- The employer is not registered with MOHRE (some startups operate through accelerators or holding entities — clarify before accepting).
- You failed the medical (no exceptions, no appeal — exit required).
- You have an active UAE labour ban from a previous employer.

## [H2] The full conversion process in 2026

This is the actual step-by-step. Each step is initiated by your employer, but you have to provide documents and attend appointments.

### [H3] Step 1: Offer letter and MOHRE contract

Your employer issues the MOHRE-compliant offer letter through the Tas-heel system. You sign it via UAE PASS or in person. This is a legally binding offer — make sure the basic salary, job title, and contract term match what you negotiated. Read our guide on [UAE Employment Contract Types](/blog/uae-employment-contract-types/) if anything looks unfamiliar.

**Your part:** Read the offer carefully. Confirm basic salary, housing allowance, transport allowance, working hours, weekly off, and probation duration.

### [H3] Step 2: Quota approval

The employer applies to MOHRE for a quota for your job title. If they already have unused quota for that title, this step is instant. If not, MOHRE reviews based on the company's labour-banking points, Emiratisation compliance, and historical hiring.

**Timeline:** 1–3 working days. **Your part:** none.

### [H3] Step 3: Work permit (pink permit)

Once quota is approved, MOHRE issues the work permit, sometimes called the "pink permit" because of its colour. This permit is valid for 60 days and is what triggers the status change.

**Cost (employer-paid):** AED 250–500 depending on skill category. **Timeline:** 2–5 working days. **Your part:** provide passport copy, photo, and educational certificates (some categories require attested degrees).

### [H3] Step 4: Status change (in-country)

Your employer's PRO submits the status change application to ICP/GDRFA. This converts your visit visa into a "status change pending" record without you leaving the country. You receive an entry permit number electronically.

**Cost (employer-paid):** AED 750–1,500. **Timeline:** 2–4 working days. **Your part:** none, but stay in the UAE — leaving mid-process voids the in-country conversion.

### [H3] Step 5: Medical fitness test

You attend a government-approved medical fitness centre. The test screens for HIV, Hepatitis B, TB, syphilis, and pregnancy (women only, for some categories). You give a blood sample and chest X-ray. Results come back in 1–3 working days.

**Cost:** AED 320 (Dubai) / AED 250 (other emirates), often paid by you upfront and reimbursed by the employer. **Centres:** DHA-approved (Dubai), SEHA (Abu Dhabi), MOHAP (other emirates). **Your part:** book the appointment, bring your passport and entry permit number. Fast-track (results in 24h) costs around AED 750.

**This is where most failures happen.** If you test positive for HIV or active TB, you are denied residence and given 30 days to exit. There is no appeal mechanism. Hepatitis B is sometimes negotiable for certain professions (food, healthcare, education are stricter). If you have any concern, get tested privately in your home country first — a positive result there is something you can plan around, not a deportation order.

### [H3] Step 6: Emirates ID biometrics

You attend an ICP centre (Tasheel or ICA) for Emirates ID biometrics — fingerprints, iris scan, photograph. Your Emirates ID application is submitted in the same visit.

**Cost (employer-paid):** AED 270 for 2-year ID + AED 100 typing fee. **Timeline:** Emirates ID is mailed in 5–10 working days, but you get a temporary printed copy of the Emirates ID number immediately. **Your part:** book the appointment (employer's PRO usually does this), attend with your passport.

### [H3] Step 7: Residence visa stamping

Your residence visa is electronically stamped into the ICP system (the physical passport stamp is no longer done — UAE moved to digital-only in 2022). You receive a residence permit number that links to your passport.

**Cost (employer-paid):** AED 250–500. **Timeline:** 1–2 working days after medical clearance and Emirates ID submission.

### [H3] Step 8: Labour card issuance

MOHRE issues the labour card linking you to the employer. You receive a notification through the MOHRE app. At this point you are a fully registered UAE resident with the right to work for that specific employer.

**Timeline:** Same day as residence stamping.

## [H2] Total cost breakdown

Numbers vary by emirate, profession, and whether your employer is in a free zone (free zone costs differ — typically higher). Mainland Dubai average for 2026:

| Item | Cost (AED) | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| MOHRE work permit | 250–500 | Employer |
| Status change | 750–1,500 | Employer |
| Medical fitness | 320 | You (often reimbursed) |
| Emirates ID (2 years) | 370 | Employer |
| Residence stamping | 250–500 | Employer |
| PRO service fees | 300–800 | Employer |
| Insurance (mandatory) | 600–1,500/year | Employer |
| **Total** | **~AED 5,500–8,500** | Mostly employer |

If you are joining a company that asks you to pay any of the employer-paid items above, push back. Under UAE Labour Law these costs are the employer's responsibility, not the candidate's. ## [H2] Common pitfalls

- **Leaving the UAE mid-process.** Don't take a weekend trip to Oman thinking the process pauses. Status change is in-country only; if you exit, the process voids and you re-enter on a fresh visit visa.
- **Educational certificate attestation.** Some skill categories (engineer, accountant, doctor, teacher) require degree attestation from the UAE Embassy in your home country AND the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFAIC). If you didn't do this before arriving, you'll be stuck. Sort it during the visit-visa period if you can.
- **Outdated passport copy.** If your passport expires within 6 months, ICP will reject the application. Renew first.
- **Insurance gap.** Employer must arrange mandatory health insurance before residence is stamped. If they're slow, you can't complete the process.
- **Old labour ban.** If you previously worked in the UAE and left under a ban, that ban must be lifted before a new permit can be issued. Check your ban status on the MOHRE app under "Bans" before signing a new offer.

## [H2] Frequently Asked Questions

### [H3] Can I work while my status change is pending?

No. You can only legally work after the labour card is issued (Step 8). Some employers will ask you to "start" during the pending period — this is a grey area and any incident during that window is unprotected.

### [H3] Do I need to do anything if my employer's PRO handles everything?

You still need to: sign the MOHRE offer via UAE PASS, attend the medical fitness appointment, attend the Emirates ID biometrics appointment, and provide passport scans and photos. The PRO does the paperwork; you provide the physical presence.

### [H3] What if my visit visa expires during the conversion?

If the work permit is issued and the status change application is filed before the visit visa expires, you are protected — the in-country status change supersedes the visit visa. If you missed the deadline, you'll pay AED 100/day overstay until exit or renewal.

### [H3] Can my family convert with me?

No — family members on visit visas must wait until your residence is fully stamped, then you sponsor them as dependants. Sponsorship requires you to have an active residence visa, salary above the minimum threshold, and a tenancy contract.

### [H3] Does this process work for free zone employers?

The high-level flow is the same, but the issuing authority is your free zone (DIFC, DMCC, ADGM, JAFZA, etc.) instead of MOHRE. Free zone permits are usually faster but more expensive. Check with your employer's PRO for free zone–specific timelines.

### [H3] Can I switch employers within the same visit visa to work visa conversion?

No. The work permit is tied to one employer at a time. To switch, you'd need to cancel the original employer's process (rarely a clean exit) or wait until your first labour card is issued and then process a cancellation + new permit with the new employer.
