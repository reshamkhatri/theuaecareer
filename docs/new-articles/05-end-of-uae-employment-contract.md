## Article body

### [PARAGRAPH] Intro

Leaving a UAE job — whether you resigned, completed your contract term, or were let go — triggers a sequence of legal and administrative steps that have hard deadlines. Get them right and you walk away with your gratuity, a clean labour record, and the option to take a new UAE role immediately. Get them wrong and you could face a 6–12 month labour ban, a chunk of unpaid gratuity, or visa overstay fines. This guide walks through the entire process day by day, from the moment you give notice to the day your last residence permit expires.

### [PARAGRAPH] Quick takeaways bullet list

- The minimum notice period in the UAE is **30 days** unless your contract specifies longer. Probation notice is 14 days (employer) or 14–30 days (employee, depending on destination).
- Gratuity is calculated on **basic salary**, not gross. First 5 years earn 21 days/year; beyond 5 years, 30 days/year. Maximum is 2 years' total basic salary.
- Your employer must pay your final settlement **within 14 days** of the last working day. If they delay, you can file a MOHRE complaint.
- After visa cancellation, you have **30 days** to either secure a new visa or exit the UAE. Some categories now get **60 days** under the 2023 rule update.
- Most labour bans were abolished by the 2021 Labour Law. The few remaining apply mainly to non-completion of contract without valid reason or unprofessional conduct.

## [H2] Day 0: The day you give notice (or get notice)

The clock starts on the day notice is delivered in writing.

If you're resigning, your notice should be:

- **In writing** (email is acceptable but a signed letter is stronger)
- Addressed to your direct manager AND HR
- State the **last working day** explicitly (not just "30 days from today")
- Request acknowledgement in writing

A simple resignation email:

> Dear [Manager], I am writing to formally resign from my position as [Role] at [Company], effective [last working day], in line with the 30-day notice period in my contract. I will use the notice period to complete handover of [X, Y, Z]. Please acknowledge receipt of this notice. Best regards, [Name].

If the employer is initiating termination, they must give written notice with at least the contractual notice period. Verbal terminations are not legally valid.

## [H2] Days 1–30: The notice period

During the notice period:

- You continue to work normally and get paid normally
- The employer can ask you to serve garden leave (paid, but not in office) — this is legal as long as you're paid
- You can request up to **8 hours/week of paid time off** to attend job interviews (Article 39 of the UAE Labour Law) — most employers don't object but it must be requested in writing
- Your annual leave balance continues to accrue
- Any pending bonus or commission earned during this period is still yours

**Do not start a new job during notice** — even if your new employer wants you to start early. You are still legally employed by your current company and double employment without proper permits is a violation.

## [H2] Day 30 (or last working day): Final day on the job

On the last working day:

- Complete your handover — get it in writing (a signed handover document protects you)
- Return company assets: laptop, phone, ID badge, car (if company car), keys
- Collect a **Service Certificate** (also called Experience Letter) — this is legally required from the employer under the UAE Labour Law and you'll need it for your next visa application
- Ask HR for confirmation of your **final settlement amount** including gratuity calculation breakdown
- Confirm the date the visa cancellation will be submitted to MOHRE

If the employer refuses to issue a service certificate or delays the final settlement, file a complaint via the MOHRE call centre on 600 590 000 or the [Tawasul portal](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/). Read our [how to file a MOHRE complaint guide](/blog/how-to-file-mohre-complaint-in-uae/) for the step-by-step.

## [H2] Days 1–14 after last working day: Final settlement payment

UAE Labour Law requires the employer to pay your final settlement within **14 days** of the last working day. The settlement should include:

- **Unpaid salary** for any worked days in the final month
- **Unused annual leave encashment** at basic + housing rate
- **Gratuity** calculated on basic salary
- **Any pending bonus or commission** earned before the last day
- **Notice payment** if the employer terminated without giving notice
- **Any deductions** for unreturned items, advances, or contractual penalties (these must be itemised)

The payment is usually transferred to your same salary-transfer bank account. Get the breakdown in writing — if any number looks off, query it before signing the cancellation paperwork.

## [H2] How your gratuity is actually calculated (2026 formula)

Under the 2021 Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021) and 2022 implementing regulations:

- **First 5 years of service:** 21 days of basic salary per year
- **Beyond 5 years:** 30 days of basic salary per year
- **Maximum total gratuity:** 2 years of total basic salary
- **Calculation base:** basic salary on the day you leave (not joining salary, not gross)

**Example:** Basic salary AED 6,000, total service 7 years.
- Years 1–5: 5 × 21 days × (AED 6,000 / 30) = 5 × 21 × 200 = **AED 21,000**
- Years 6–7: 2 × 30 days × (AED 6,000 / 30) = 2 × 30 × 200 = **AED 12,000**
- **Total gratuity: AED 33,000**

Under the new law, employees who **resign from a fixed-term contract after completing 1 year receive full gratuity**. Previously, resignation reduced gratuity to a fraction. This is the single biggest change candidates miss — check your calculation against the new rule, not the old one.

Use the [Gratuity Calculator](/tools/gratuity-calculator/) to verify the number before accepting the settlement.

## [H2] Day 14 (or earlier): Visa cancellation submitted

The employer's PRO submits the work permit cancellation through MOHRE Tas-heel. This triggers two things:

1. Your **labour card is cancelled** — you can no longer legally work for that employer
2. Your **residence visa is cancelled** — you receive an SMS from ICP and have a fixed grace period (see next section)

Before signing the cancellation form, double-check:

- The reason for cancellation matches reality (resignation, mutual termination, end of contract, etc.)
- Your final settlement has been paid
- You have received your service certificate

**Do not sign cancellation paperwork before receiving the settlement.** Once cancellation is processed, your leverage drops dramatically.

## [H2] Day 0 (visa cancelled) to Day 30/60: Grace period

After your residence visa is cancelled, you have a grace period to either:

- **Get a new residence visa** sponsored by a new employer
- **Convert to a different sponsor** (spouse, parent, etc.)
- **Exit the UAE**

The grace period in 2026 is:

- **30 days** standard
- **60 days** for certain skill categories (skilled professionals under ICP's updated rules)
- **180 days** if you held an investor or Golden Visa (different mechanism — visa is not auto-cancelled with employment)

If you overstay past the grace period, you incur AED 50/day overstay fines, and after a long overstay, an entry ban can be issued.

## [H2] Days 0–60: Finding the next job

If you have a job lined up:

- Your new employer's PRO will process your new work permit using the cancellation letter from your previous employer
- The conversion can happen without leaving the country
- Read our guide on [UAE visit visa to work visa conversion](/blog/uae-visit-visa-to-work-visa-conversion/) — the in-country status change works similarly for visa-to-visa transfers

If you're still searching:

- Update your CV — use the [free CV Maker](/tools/cv-maker/)
- Browse current openings on the [UAE jobs board](/jobs/) and [walk-in interviews](/jobs/walk-in/)
- Apply to as many roles as you can within the first 30 days — the longer the gap, the harder the next conversation

## [H2] Labour bans in 2026: what's actually banned

The 2021 Labour Law abolished most automatic labour bans. As of 2026, you face a ban only in narrow cases:

- **Leaving during probation** without serving the proper notice — typically a 1-year ban
- **Abandoning the job** (absent without leave for 7+ consecutive days) — 1-year ban
- **Dismissal for gross misconduct** under Article 44 — up to 1-year ban
- **Working illegally** for a different employer during your contract — 1-year ban and potentially deportation

You do NOT face an automatic ban for:

- Resigning with proper notice
- Completing a fixed-term contract
- Mutual separation
- Being made redundant
- Switching to another sponsor

If you suspect your employer is trying to place an unjustified ban, you can challenge it through the MOHRE conciliation process before the ban is finalised.

## [H2] Final settlement: the banking side

Once your settlement hits your account, before you leave the UAE:

- **Settle outstanding personal loans or credit cards.** UAE banks can place travel bans for unpaid debt — even small balances. Resolve before exit.
- **Close or transition your bank account.** If you're leaving permanently, close the account properly. If you're switching employers, ask the bank for a 90-day grace before they auto-close for non-salary credit.
- **Cancel utility accounts (DEWA, etisalat/du, SEWA, FEWA).** Get final bills and settle.
- **Transfer or cancel your tenancy contract.** Use Ejari to officially terminate; otherwise the rent obligation continues.
- **Cancel insurance, gym, school fees** — anything on standing instruction.

These are the items that catch people on the way out and cost more than the gratuity windfall.

## [H2] Frequently Asked Questions

### [H3] What if my employer refuses to pay my gratuity?

File a MOHRE complaint on 600 590 000 or via the Tawasul portal. MOHRE will summon both parties for conciliation. If no resolution within 14 days, the case is referred to the Labour Court. Bring your contract, MOHRE-registered salary certificate, and resignation letter as evidence. See our [how to file a MOHRE complaint guide](/blog/how-to-file-mohre-complaint-in-uae/) for full steps.

### [H3] Can I leave the UAE during the grace period without losing my new job offer?

Yes, but be careful with timing. If you exit during the grace period, you generally re-enter on a new visa sponsored by the new employer (in-country conversion ends when you exit). Coordinate with both employers' PROs before booking flights.

### [H3] My contract was 2 years and I'm leaving after 18 months. Do I still get gratuity?

Yes. Under the 2021 law, fixed-term contracts pay full gratuity after 1 year of service regardless of whether you complete the full term. The old rule that reduced gratuity for early exit no longer applies.

### [H3] What happens to my family's residence visas when mine is cancelled?

Your family members' visas are linked to yours. When yours is cancelled, theirs are cancelled too, and they enter the same grace period. If you find a new job within the grace period, you can re-sponsor them under your new residence visa.

### [H3] Do I have to pay back any joining bonus if I leave within the first year?

Only if your signed contract has a clawback clause. Read the offer letter and MOHRE contract carefully. Some companies pro-rate the clawback; some require full repayment if you leave within 12 months. Don't assume — check the document.

### [H3] My employer is asking me to sign a "no claim" form. Should I?

A "no claim" form (sometimes called a Final Settlement and Release) is normal at the end of a settlement — you confirm you've received everything owed and waive future claims. Only sign after you've received and verified every component of the settlement (salary, leave, gratuity, bonus). Once signed, you typically cannot reopen the matter.

### [H3] Can I get my Emirates ID kept active after visa cancellation?

No — your Emirates ID is tied to your residence visa. Once the visa is cancelled, the EID is automatically cancelled too. If you secure a new visa within the grace period, you'll be issued a new EID (free of charge in most renewal scenarios).
